# 828 Construction Design Direction

Last updated: May 18, 2026

## Client Direction

Joe wants the 828 Construction website to feel premium, custom, professional, and visually memorable. It should not feel like a generic contractor template or AI-generated construction site.

Important client preferences:

- Website elements should "pop" visually while still feeling expensive and restrained.
- Use elegant split-section layouts often: strong image or construction object on one side, clean text on the other.
- Major headlines should use a large editorial serif style, similar to luxury product websites.
- The visual tone should be premium, modern, masculine, clean, trustworthy, and construction related.
- Use subtle copper accents where appropriate.
- Do not overload the site. Depth, contrast, and visual interest should feel deliberate.
- Drafting details, rulers, protractors, grids, and technical linework are part of the custom 828 language, but they should be placed selectively as intentional section systems rather than repeated on every section.
- The client does not want too many sections. Customers need meaningful, decisive sections, not a long page full of repeated words. Every section must have a clear job: establish trust, explain a service, show proof, or convert.

## Research Notes

Modern premium contractor, remodeling, and luxury builder websites tend to rely on:

- Full-bleed or oversized project photography.
- Alternating image/text layouts with concise, confident copy.
- Editorial typography paired with restrained sans serif body text.
- Clear trust markers such as license, years of experience, awards, reviews, and process clarity.
- Smooth scroll reveals, image parallax, and masked image entries rather than flashy effects.
- Layered material details: plans, tools, wood, stone, metal, or architectural fragments used as accents.
- Premium animation density works best when there is one strong signature motion moment, supported by calmer sections around it.

Useful inspiration categories researched:

- Luxury custom home builders.
- Design-build remodeling firms.
- High-end ADU and residential construction portfolios.
- Contractor sites that emphasize clarity, trust, craft, and process.

## Visual Asset Direction

Prioritize full photographic scenes over isolated decorative props:

- Finished exterior and ADU photography.
- Real plan-review and estimating table imagery.
- In-progress framing, remediation, and material detail photography.
- Clean detail crops of tile, concrete, stucco, waterproofing, and hardware.
- Subtle architectural linework only when it supports the composition and does not cross important text.

Avoid obvious generated cutouts, floating random tools, cartoon-like object props, low-quality AI artifacts, clutter, and repeated decorative assets. Decorative objects should never look pasted on. If a transparent PNG is used, it needs a specific art-directed purpose, unique placement, and visual QA at desktop and mobile sizes before it stays.

Post-feedback note: a previous iteration used generated floating cutout PNGs. That made the site feel less professional and should not be repeated.

Post-feedback note 2: another iteration over-applied drafting/instrument overlays across too many sections. Keep the ruler/protractor/grid language, but use it with restraint and test placement section by section.

## Animation Direction

Animations should be smooth, subtle, and premium:

- Fade in and slide up on section entry.
- Masked image reveals.
- Gentle parallax for background images and transparent PNG objects.
- Horizontal or pinned motion only when it improves the story.

Avoid cheesy bounces, excessive rotation, distracting movement, and anything that interferes with reading or conversion.
