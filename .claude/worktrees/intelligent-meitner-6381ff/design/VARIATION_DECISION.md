# Variation Decision — Projects Section Redesign

## Scoring matrix (5 criteria, 1–5 per variation)

| Criterion | Variation A: Asymmetric Grid | Variation B: Film Strip | Variation C: Punch 3-Col Grid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero dead space | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| Continuous scroll motion | 4 | 5 | 4 |
| Editorial sophistication | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| Full-bleed photography | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| Mobile graceful at 390px | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| **Total** | **23** | **22** | **20** |

## Winner: Variation A — Asymmetric Editorial Grid

Variation A wins on editorial sophistication and mobile graceful — the two criteria that matter most for a premium construction brand. The 8/4-column split creates a hierarchy that reads as intentional curation, not gallery display. The two secondary cards stacking to exactly match the featured card's height produces a precise geometric relationship that signals craft. On mobile, the collapse to full-width single column is clean and maintains image prominence.

Variation B was close. Its vertical film strip with alternating image/text sides and numbered rows is dramatic and continuous. It lost on editorial sophistication (it reads more like a timeline than a curated portfolio) and on the dead space criterion (the text panels create voids alongside images at certain aspect ratios).

Variation C's 3-column uniform grid is the most common portfolio layout on the web. It has zero dead space and clean mobile behavior, but it adds nothing distinctive. The punch-in clip-path entrance (which Variation A also uses) is its strongest point, but the equal-size cards lack the hierarchy that a featured/secondary system provides.

## Footer Winner: Variation A — Stacked Editorial

Large phone number as the dominant element, inline flex-wrap navigation, clear section labels in tracked caps, full-width CTA. This pattern comes from NS Builders' mobile footer approach. It's the clearest expression of the site's design language at small screens.
